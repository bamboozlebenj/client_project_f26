During Your Experience

Navigating Your Active Project

Use this guide to manage projects and teams, conduct human-centered research, communicate with partners, respond to feedback, and adapt when the project changes.

The Value of Navigating Friction

Real-world projects rarely follow a straight line. Requirements can shift, team dynamics can evolve, clients can face unexpected constraints, and research can produce surprising results.

The goal during this phase is not to avoid every challenge. Instead, use challenges as opportunities to practice adaptability, ethical decision-making, communication, and problem-solving.

Phase 3: Managing & Executing in the Field

Conduct research, track progress, and maintain clear communication with project sponsors and partners.

Focus & Objectives

  • Fieldwork & Research Methods: Apply human-centered design tools to observe contexts and extract insights.
  • Sponsor & Client Updates: Maintain transparency with regular progress reporting.
  • Feedback Loops: Gather, evaluate, and integrate constructive feedback during the project.

When the Project Changes

  1. Document what changed and why it matters.
  2. Discuss the change with the people affected by it.
  3. Revisit the project scope, timeline, and responsibilities.
  4. Use feedback to identify what should change and what should remain stable.
  5. Communicate the updated plan to your team and partner.
