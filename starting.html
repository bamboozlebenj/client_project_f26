Starting Your Experience

Turn Preparation into a Strong Start

The starting phase is where project ideas become structured commitments. Focus on professional communication, productive first meetings, clear roles, and shared expectations with your team and partner.

Why the Start Matters

First impressions can shape the trajectory of a partnership. Establishing clear team agreements, creating useful meeting agendas, and aligning on project scope early can help build trust with your client and clarity within your team.

Early ambiguity is a normal part of engaged learning. Use the beginning of the experience to ask questions, clarify responsibilities, and document what everyone understands about the project.

Phase 2: Starting the Experience

Partner Communication

Draft clear, professional outreach to launch your working relationship with a client or community partner.

  • Introduce your team and explain the purpose of the project.
  • Confirm the best communication channels and expected response times.
  • Use your early conversations to identify questions that need clarification.

Plan the First Meeting

Use a kickoff meeting to establish a shared understanding of the project and the people involved.

  1. Introduce everyone involved in the project.
  2. Review the project's purpose and current understanding of the scope.
  3. Discuss roles, responsibilities, deadlines, and communication practices.
  4. Identify open questions and decide who will follow up.
  5. Confirm next steps before ending the meeting.

Formalize Agreements

Document important commitments so students, instructors, and partners have a shared reference point for the project.

  • Team responsibilities and leadership roles
  • Project scope and expected deliverables
  • Meeting cadence and communication channels
  • Important milestones and deadlines
  • Questions or decisions that still need to be resolved

Questions to Answer Before Moving Forward

  • What is our shared understanding of the project scope?
  • Who is responsible for each major part of the project?
  • How and when will our team communicate with the partner?
  • What decisions require partner input?
  • What are our next concrete steps?
  • What questions remain unanswered?
